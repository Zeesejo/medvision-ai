\begin{abstract}
Automated analysis of chest radiographs is a critical step toward
scalable clinical decision support. We present \textbf{MedVision-AI},
a multi-label classification framework for detecting 14 thoracic
pathologies in chest X-rays. Our approach combines a DenseNet-121
backbone pretrained on ImageNet with an Asymmetric Loss (ASL)
function that addresses severe class imbalance inherent in the NIH
ChestX-ray14 dataset (112{,}120 images, 14 labels). To enhance
clinical interpretability, we integrate Gradient-weighted Class
Activation Mapping (Grad-CAM), producing class-specific heatmaps
that highlight pathologically relevant lung regions. Through a
systematic backbone ablation, we show that DenseNet-121 + ASL
achieves a mean AUC of \textbf{0.7978} across all 14 classes,
outperforming both a ResNet-50 + ASL baseline (mean AUC 0.7067)
and the original Wang et~al.\ benchmark (mean AUC 0.7380).
We further release a lightweight Gradio-based clinical interface
enabling real-time inference and visualization.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Chest radiography is the most widely performed diagnostic imaging
procedure worldwide, yet interpreting radiographs requires significant
clinical expertise~\cite{rajpurkar2017chexnet}. Automated detection
systems based on deep learning have shown promise in assisting
radiologists, particularly in resource-limited settings where specialist
access is scarce~\cite{wang2017chestxray14}.

A key challenge is the \textit{multi-label} nature of the task:
a single radiograph may exhibit multiple concurrent pathologies.
Furthermore, the NIH ChestX-ray14 dataset~\cite{wang2017chestxray14}
suffers from severe class imbalance --- pathologies such as Hernia
and Pneumonia appear in fewer than 1\% of images, while \emph{No
Finding} accounts for over 50\% of the dataset. Standard binary
cross-entropy loss treats all misclassifications equally, causing
models to degenerate toward majority-class predictions and
suppressing recall on rare but clinically critical pathologies.

A second critical challenge is \textit{clinical interpretability}.
Black-box predictions are insufficient for deployment in medical
settings; clinicians require visual justification of model
decisions. Grad-CAM~\cite{selvaraju2017gradcam} addresses this by
producing class-discriminative heatmaps localizing image regions
most influential to a prediction, enabling radiologists to verify
model attention without architectural modification.

Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
  \item A systematic backbone ablation comparing ResNet-50 and
        DenseNet-121 under identical ASL training conditions,
        demonstrating a +9.1\% mean AUC gain for DenseNet-121.
  \item Asymmetric Loss integration that significantly reduces
        the class-collapse effect observed with standard BCE,
        recovering near-chance performance on minority classes.
  \item Integration of Grad-CAM for class-specific visual
        explanations, providing clinically interpretable
        localization maps.
  \item A comprehensive per-class AUC evaluation on NIH
        ChestX-ray14, surpassing the Wang et~al.\ baseline
        by +6.0 mean AUC points.
  \item A public Gradio interface for interactive clinical
        inference and visualization.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Chest X-ray Classification.}
Wang et~al.~\cite{wang2017chestxray14} introduced ChestX-ray14 and
established baseline AUCs using a DenseNet-121 variant, reporting
a mean AUC of 0.738 across 14 pathologies.
CheXNet~\cite{rajpurkar2017chexnet} improved upon this benchmark
with a 121-layer DenseNet, demonstrating radiologist-level
performance on pneumonia detection. Subsequent work explored
attention mechanisms~\cite{chen2019attention}, graph-based label
correlations~\cite{chen2019gcn}, and vision
transformers~\cite{dosovitskiy2020vit} for multi-label
classification. More recent approaches leveraging EfficientNet
and ViT backbones have reported mean AUCs in the range 0.83--0.85
on ChestX-ray14~\cite{ma2021vit}.

\paragraph{Class Imbalance.}
Focal Loss~\cite{lin2017focal} down-weights easy negatives during
training, improving performance on imbalanced datasets. Asymmetric
Loss~\cite{ridnik2021asl} extends this idea by decoupling the
focusing parameters for positive (\(\gamma^+ = 0\)) and negative
(\(\gamma^- = 4\)) samples, also applying a probability margin
\(m = 0.05\) to shift hard negatives toward zero. On multi-label
benchmarks such as MS-COCO and Pascal VOC, ASL achieves
state-of-the-art results with minimal hyperparameter sensitivity.

\paragraph{Explainability.}
Grad-CAM~\cite{selvaraju2017gradcam} generates class-discriminative
localization maps by weighting convolutional feature maps with the
gradient of the target class score, without requiring architectural
modification or retraining. Recent work has validated Grad-CAM
heatmaps against radiologist annotations on chest X-ray datasets,
confirming anatomical alignment for pathologies with well-localized
appearance such as cardiomegaly, effusion, and pneumothorax.

\section{Methodology}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Dataset}
The NIH ChestX-ray14 dataset~\cite{wang2017chestxray14} contains
112{,}120 frontal-view chest X-rays from 30{,}805 unique patients,
annotated with 14 disease labels extracted from radiology reports
via NLP. We follow the official patient-level train/test split
(77{,}872 train / 8{,}652 validation / 25{,}596 test), ensuring
no patient appears in both train and test sets. All images are
resized to \(320 \times 320\) pixels during evaluation; training
uses random cropping to \(224 \times 224\), horizontal flipping,
and color jitter as augmentation.

\subsection{Model Architecture}
We evaluate two backbone architectures under identical training
conditions:

\paragraph{ResNet-50.} A 50-layer residual network~\cite{he2016resnet}
pretrained on ImageNet. The backbone produces a
\(\mathbf{z} \in \mathbb{R}^{2048}\) global average-pooled
feature vector.

\paragraph{DenseNet-121.} A 121-layer densely connected
network~\cite{huang2017densenet} pretrained on ImageNet, with
7.5M parameters. The backbone produces a
\(\mathbf{z} \in \mathbb{R}^{1024}\) feature vector.

In both cases the classification head is:
\begin{equation}
  \hat{y} = \sigma\!\left( W_2 \cdot \text{GELU}(W_1 \cdot
  \text{LN}(\mathbf{z})) \right)
  \label{eq:head}
\end{equation}
where \(\text{LN}\) denotes Layer Normalization, dropout
(\(p=0.3\)) is applied after the hidden layer, and \(\sigma\)
is the sigmoid activation applied element-wise to produce
independent per-class probabilities.

\subsection{Loss Function}
To address class imbalance, we adopt Asymmetric
Loss~\cite{ridnik2021asl}:
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}_{\text{ASL}} = -\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i}\sum_{c}
  \Bigl[ y_{ic}\,(1-p_{ic})^{\gamma^+}\log p_{ic} +
  (1-y_{ic})\,(p^-_{ic})^{\gamma^-}\log(1-p^-_{ic}) \Bigr]
  \label{eq:asl}
\end{equation}
where \(p^-_{ic} = \max(p_{ic} - m,\, 0)\) applies a probability
margin \(m=0.05\) to shift hard negatives, with \(\gamma^+=0\)
and \(\gamma^-=4\). The asymmetry between positive and negative
gradients prevents the model from collapsing to the all-negative
prediction, which is the primary failure mode of standard BCE
on heavily imbalanced multi-label problems.

\subsection{Training Protocol}
We train for 30 epochs using AdamW~\cite{loshchilov2019adamw}
with learning rate \(10^{-4}\), weight decay \(10^{-4}\), and
cosine annealing with 3 warmup epochs. Gradient clipping at
\(\ell_2\)-norm 1.0 is applied. Mixed-precision training (AMP)
is enabled throughout. Early stopping with patience 7 monitors
validation mean AUC. The best checkpoint is selected by peak
validation AUC; for ResNet-50 this was epoch 15, for DenseNet-121
epoch 30.

\subsection{Grad-CAM Visualization}
Grad-CAM~\cite{selvaraju2017gradcam} computes the gradient of
the class score \(y^c\) with respect to the feature maps
\(A^k\) of the final convolutional layer:
\begin{equation}
  \alpha_k^c = \frac{1}{Z}\sum_i\sum_j
  \frac{\partial y^c}{\partial A^k_{ij}}
  \label{eq:gradcam_weights}
\end{equation}
The localization map is then:
\begin{equation}
  L^c_{\text{Grad-CAM}} = \text{ReLU}\!\left(
  \sum_k \alpha_k^c A^k \right)
  \label{eq:gradcam_map}
\end{equation}
This produces class-specific heatmaps highlighting diagnostically
relevant regions. For DenseNet-121 we target the output of the
final dense block (\texttt{denseblock4}); for ResNet-50 we
target \texttt{layer4}.

\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Evaluation Metric}
Following standard practice~\cite{wang2017chestxray14}, we report
the Area Under the ROC Curve (AUC) for each of the 14 pathology
classes on the official test set (25{,}596 images) and compute
the unweighted mean AUC across all classes.

\subsection{Quantitative Results}

Table~\ref{tab:auc} presents per-class AUC for both backbones
alongside the Wang et~al.\ baseline. DenseNet-121 + ASL achieves
a mean AUC of \textbf{0.7978}, outperforming both ResNet-50 + ASL
(0.7067, $\Delta = +0.091$) and the original Wang et~al.\
benchmark (0.738, $\Delta = +0.060$). Notable improvements are
observed for Emphysema (0.916), Hernia (0.891), Pneumothorax
(0.857), and Cardiomegaly (0.869) --- pathologies with visually
distinct and well-localized radiographic appearance.

\paragraph{ResNet-50 Collapse Analysis.}
ResNet-50 exhibits near-chance AUC (0.500) on Atelectasis,
Effusion, and Infiltration --- the three most prevalent positive
classes. Inspection of the saved checkpoint (epoch 15, validation
mean AUC 0.716) confirms the model converged but learned to
predict the all-negative response for these high-frequency
classes, a known failure mode when the negative gradient
dominates despite ASL. DenseNet-121's denser feature reuse
and more discriminative bottleneck features appear to provide
sufficient gradient signal to avoid this collapse.

\subsection{Qualitative Analysis}

Grad-CAM heatmaps computed on DenseNet-121's final dense block
demonstrate anatomically plausible attention for pathologies
with well-defined spatial signatures. For Cardiomegaly, the
model consistently highlights the cardiac silhouette region.
For Pneumothorax, attention localizes to the peripheral lung
fields. For Hernia, activation concentrates at the diaphragmatic
boundary --- consistent with radiological localization of
diaphragmatic hernias.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

Our results confirm two central hypotheses. First, DenseNet-121's
dense connectivity pattern --- where each layer receives feature
maps from all preceding layers --- provides richer gradient paths
than ResNet-50's residual shortcuts, translating to higher AUC
across 13 of 14 pathologies under identical training conditions.
Second, Asymmetric Loss substantially mitigates class collapse
for DenseNet-121: all 14 classes achieve AUC well above chance,
whereas ResNet-50 collapses on the three most prevalent minority
classes despite the same loss formulation. This suggests backbone
capacity and feature diversity interact non-trivially with
imbalance-aware loss functions.

The absolute mean AUC of 0.7978 places our method above the
Wang et~al.\ baseline (0.738) and in the competitive range of
contemporary single-backbone methods reported in the literature
(0.80--0.85)~\cite{ma2021vit}. The gap to state-of-the-art
transformer-based ensembles (0.85+) is primarily attributable
to the absence of explicit label dependency modeling and the
well-documented label noise in ChestX-ray14~\cite{oakden2020hidden},
which fundamentally limits the ceiling AUC achievable by any
single-model approach.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
ChestX-ray14 labels were extracted via NLP from radiology reports
and may contain significant noise~\cite{oakden2020hidden};
a study by Oakden-Rayner et~al.\ found label error rates up
to 50\% for some pathologies. Additionally, our model does not
explicitly model label correlations (e.g., Effusion and
Consolidation co-occur frequently), leaving structured prediction
gains unexplored.

\paragraph{Future Work.}
Promising directions include: (1) incorporating Vision Transformers
or hybrid CNN-ViT backbones to capture long-range spatial
dependencies; (2) modelling label dependencies via graph neural
networks; (3) multi-dataset validation on MIMIC-CXR and CheXpert
to assess generalizability; and (4) fairness analysis across
patient demographics (age, sex) to surface and mitigate
performance disparities.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented MedVision-AI, an explainable multi-label chest X-ray
classification framework combining DenseNet-121, Asymmetric Loss,
and Grad-CAM visualization. Through a systematic backbone ablation
we demonstrated that DenseNet-121 + ASL achieves a mean AUC of
0.7978 on NIH ChestX-ray14, surpassing the original Wang et~al.\
benchmark by +6.0 AUC points and outperforming the ResNet-50
baseline by +9.1 points. Grad-CAM heatmaps provide
anatomically plausible localization for pathologies with distinct
spatial signatures. The accompanying Gradio interface enables
interactive clinical deployment without specialized infrastructure.
Our code, trained checkpoints, and evaluation scripts are
publicly available at \url{https://github.com/Zeesejo/medvision-ai}.
